Part 2 • Your Starting Point

Let's explore your career mobility.

New • Existing Occupational Value

What can your experience already do?

Before looking at a new career, BOOST checks whether the occupation you already know connects to in-demand work. These are occupationally similar H2/H3/+ opportunities based on the technical skill profile and preparation level of your current/recent occupation.

Why this matters: A new career interest does not automatically mean new training is needed. Existing occupational skills may already support direct employment or a nearby career move.

Bring forward your O*NET Interest Profiler scores from Module 1

Interest is a preference signal—not a readiness score. These scores help BOOST show which kinds of work may appeal to you. They do not determine whether you are qualified, whether an occupation is in demand, or whether training is needed.

Transferable Experience

Which skills have you actually used?

O*NET associates these skills with your current or recent occupation. Confirm how much you have actually used each one. BOOST uses this as supporting evidence for career mobility—not as proof that you need training.

Report your experience—not how good you think you are.

Your Career Mobility

Opportunities Worth Exploring

Capability and career preference are both important evidence—but they are not the same evidence. BOOST keeps occupational value, transferable experience, interest alignment, regional opportunity, and preparation distance separate so you can see whether you can work now, move now, or build toward something different.
How to read the evidence: WORK NOW reflects marketable occupational experience. MOVE NOW reflects transferable experience that may support a nearby move. BUILD TOWARD reflects attractive in-demand careers where meaningful preparation may still be required. Interest Alignment is shown across all three as a preference signal—not as proof of readiness or training need.
